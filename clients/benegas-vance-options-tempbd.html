How These Numbers Work

Why the same rate costs more nowMortgage rates move daily based on bond markets. When rates rise, previously above-par rates drop below par. 6.375% was generating a $2,252 lender credit two weeks ago — today it costs $2,533 in points to achieve. The payments are identical; it just costs more upfront to lock in that rate.
How the lender credit worksWhen you take a rate above par (6.625% or 6.750%), the lender pays you a credit at closing. That credit can be applied directly to the 2/1 buydown account, reducing what the seller and borrower need to cover. It's essentially the lender subsidizing part of your buydown in exchange for a slightly higher note rate.
The tradeoff in plain termsOption A: Lowest permanent rate ($4,200/mo) but highest cash to close ($5,704). Option B: $88/mo more permanently, but $4,335 less at closing. Option C: $133/mo more permanently, $0 cash to close and full earnest refund. Over 12 months, Option C costs $1,596 more in payments but saves $5,704 at closing — a net positive for the first 3+ years.
Gift fund breakdownIn all three options, $19,250 of the $20,000 gift covers the down payment. The remaining $750 flows toward closing costs. If the gift is $15,000, a $4,250 down payment gap is created — Options B and C are still fully covered by the $7,000 earnest even at $15K.
2/1 Buydown mechanicsSeller concessions and lender credit fund a buydown escrow account at closing. Year 1 rate is 2% below the note rate, Year 2 is 1% below, Year 3 onward is the permanent note rate. No DPA program, no second mortgage in any scenario — a single clean FHA loan.
