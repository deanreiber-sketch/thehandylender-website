How This Works

2/1 Buydown Mechanics The seller funds a buydown escrow account at closing. Year 1 your rate is reduced by 2%, Year 2 by 1%, then your permanent note rate applies from Year 3 onward. The buydown account subsidizes the difference — no temporary loan, no balloon.
The Refinance Strategy At the end of Year 2 you refinance the first mortgage and roll the DPA balance into the new loan. If rates have improved to even 5.875% — the same as your Year 2 buydown rate — your payment locks in at $3,780–3,907 permanently, well under $4,100. The refi breakeven rate is very achievable.
Repayable 2nd Mortgage The DPA second mortgage has no monthly interest — you pay 8.25% over 10 years on just the $18–19K balance. At refi it rolls into your new first mortgage. If you sell before refinancing, the balance is due at closing from proceeds.
Offer Strategy At this price point ($520–540K) homes aren't sitting. Offering $10K over asking with $13–15K in seller concessions is a competitive, clean offer that sellers understand. The seller nets asking price; you get the concessions to fund the buydown.
Scenario 1 Advantage Lower purchase price means the lender credit at par 6.875% covers a significant portion of costs — borrower brings just $5,552. Ideal if preserving cash is the priority and the $30K gift from parents stays in reserve.
Scenario 2 Advantage Slightly higher price but permanently lower rate (6.625% vs 6.875%) saves $39/mo every month from Year 3 on. Costs $14,876 at closing — well within the $20K own funds available. Gift funds can remain untouched as a cushion.
